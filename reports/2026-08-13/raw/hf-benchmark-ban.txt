THE RESEARCH THAT INVALIDATES THE ARC-C CLAIM
A 2024 paper from Snowflake AI Research (Borchmann, arXiv:2412.17758 —
"In Case You Missed It: ARC 'Challenge' Is Not That Challenging"
) directly demonstrated that ARC-C's perceived difficulty is
an artifact of the evaluation setup
, not the actual complexity of the questions:
When Llama 3.1 70B was evaluated with all answer options visible (how humans take tests), ARC-C accuracy jumped from
64% to 93%
— a 29-point swing
just from changing the format
31% of ARC-C questions are "hardly answerable in separation" — they require comparing options to answer, but get evaluated without showing all choices
OpenBookQA went from 48% to 89% with correct evaluation — meaning it's essentially "solved," and any score differences are eval artifacts
The authors' conclusion:
"The previously perceived difficulty was primarily an artifact of the evaluation method rather than the tasks' complexity"
So ARC-C was never an "intelligence barrier." It was a
formatting issue
. The "700 club" isn't some exclusive frontier model zone — it's a number that moves 29 points depending on how you set up your evaluation harness.
OpenAI, Claude, and Gemini do not compete on ARC-C.
They don't report it. They don't market around it. They compete on SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 2.0, GPQA Diamond, LiveCodeBench, AIME, and HMMT.

Legacy quants

Q4_0
has roughly
5x the perplexity error

Q4_K_M
at nearly identical file size
Legacy quants (Q4_0, Q4_1, Q5_0, Q5_1) use uniform scalar quantization — inferior to K-quants

than the equivalent K-quant

DavidAU frequently uses legacy quants (Q5_1, Q4_1) instead of K-quants (Q5_K_M, Q4_K_M) with no explanation.
