The Catalogue of Ships
In the second book of the
Iliad
the poet pauses on the brink of the first battle, calls on the Muses, and counts the fleet: twenty-nine contingents, forty-six captains, some 178 place-names, 1,186 ships. This site puts every entry on the map, rebuilds the ships from the archaeological evidence, and lets you read the lines beside the ruins they describe.

Tell me now, you Muses who have your homes on Olympus — for you are goddesses, you are present, you know all things, while we hear only rumour and know nothing — who were the leaders and lords of the Danaans. The ships
Homer's warship is a long, open, oared galley — "black", "hollow", "swift", "well-benched", "curved" — with a single square sail and a crew that doubles as its fighting force. No hull survives, but pictures do. Two reconstructions are offered here: the twelfth-century galley painted on kraters at Kynos in Locris, and the eighth-century pentekonter of the Athenian Geometric vases. Drag to orbit, click the numbered markers to fly to each part, and use the investigation tools to take the ship apart: dimensions, a cross-section, an exploded view, and the sherd drawing laid over the model.

The numbers
The ship-counts add up, exactly, to 1,186. The Catalogue gives a crew figure only twice — 120 for the Boeotians, 50 for Philoctetes — and Thucydides used the pair to estimate the army.

The 3D models
The galleys are procedural reconstructions built in Three.js for this site. Hull form, the open rowers' gallery, the bird-head stem device, the loose-footed brailed sail and the single starboard steering oar follow the twelfth-century images from Kynos, Tragana and the Sea Peoples reliefs at Medinet Habu; the deck over the rowers is Wachsmann's reading and can be switched off. The squared forefoot, horn stem, bow screen, fan-shaped stern and paired steering oars of the Geometric version follow the Athenian kraters. Dimensions (about 30 m by 4 m for a fifty-oared ship) follow Morrison and Casson. Everything not visible in an ancient image — the exact frame, the rigging runs, the colours beyond "black" and "vermilion-cheeked" — is a reasoned guess, and is labelled as such in the part descriptions.
Method
Places are plotted at their conventional identifications; where scholars disagree or the site is unknown, the marker is dashed and the entry says so. Ship counts, leaders and line numbers are checked against the Greek text reproduced on this page. The count of forty-six captains includes the dead Protesilaos and the absent Philoktetes together with the men who replaced them; forty-four are actually in command. Thucydides rounds the fleet to 1,200 ships.
