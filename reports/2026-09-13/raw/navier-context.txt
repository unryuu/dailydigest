I think I'm in love with this line.
More seriously, I do wonder very much how similar the proof structures are to Levent & Tristan's work, and if they are different, how much we can glean from that. How much of the difference may be due to "proof resampling"? Like, suppose OpenAI did train the model on their data, and then prompted it to re-generate the proof (or resolve a different but very related question). Would it produce
the same
construction? If no, how different would the construction be? I would intuitively expect them to be different on the surface (perhaps even proving somewhat different things, as OpenAI alludes), but similar in some more deep conceptual sense.
I'm not going to review several hundred pages of math PDFs because of it, though, and in any case my understanding is that the most relevant Levent & Tristan result ("blowup for hypo-dissipative Navier-Stokes") isn't out, so no side-by-side comparison is yet possible. Still, when it's out, this sort of comparison seems like one of the most reliable sources of evidence on what actually happened.
... though, in the medium-term, I suppose the question will be resolved by whether OpenAI's claims about their new internal model will turn out to be true. E. g.,
this one
by Noam Brown:
It can be hard to “feel the AGI” until you see an AI surpass you in a domain you care deeply about. This week, many mathematicians and physicists at @OpenAI had their Lee Sedol moment seeing this model solve, in minutes, open problems they’d struggled with for years.
If that is the case, it's not going to stop at Navier-Stokes.
The alternate hypothesis, which I'd usually consider highly implausible copium, but which this debacle has suddenly injected a lot of probability mass into, is that OpenAI really is engaging in massive amounts of fabrication in order to keep themselves afloat, presenting math results that were produced by humans or with heavy human involvement as autonomous AI discoveries. Tristan at least
implies
that.
[1]
... I find that I don't actually believe this, even now, but we'll see.

Hm. This is a really weird confluence of circumstances all around.

OpenAI happened to be developing a surprisingly, off-trend-powerful internal model that was
so
powerful as to (near-?)autonomously resolve the same problem in the timespan between OpenAI hearing of the rumor and the papers' publication.
I suppose weirder things have happened, but it does seem overall more plausible if we do assume some data leakage from Levent & Tristan...?
